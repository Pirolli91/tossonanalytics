## ⚡ The Top Line: NC DEQ Finds 'Significant Deficiencies' in Chemours' Consent-Order Cleanup Plan and Directs a Full Lower Cape Fear Assessment

- **Defining Number:** **≈$1.2 billion spent on Consent Order compliance · ~27,500 household wells sampled · ~10,500 families offered filtration — yet DEQ says the contamination assessment is unfinished and the long-term cleanup has stalled**
- **Primary Sources (`NC-01`, `NC-17`):** [https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10) · [https://tossonanalytics.com/regulatory-watch](https://tossonanalytics.com/regulatory-watch)

On November 9, 2026, the NC Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) sent Chemours formal comments finding significant deficiencies in the company's On and Offsite Assessment and Corrective Action Plan under the 2019 Consent Order, directing Chemours to fully assess contaminated soil and groundwater throughout the Lower Cape Fear region and any associated risks, revise its Corrective Action Plan to restore contaminated groundwater in compliance with state law, expand the plan to include all PFAS associated with Fayetteville Works rather than a limited subset of contaminants, and evaluate the impacts of the facility's remaining emissions on groundwater. 'Today's direction to the company makes clear that DEQ expects Chemours to clean up all the areas they have polluted,' said DEQ Secretary Reid Wilson. 'DEQ will hold Chemours accountable no matter how long it takes.' DEQ framed the action as a major step forward in the process outlined in the September 2026 settlement among Chemours, North Carolina, and 11 counties and municipal water systems — confirming the settlement did not close the consent-order cleanup dispute.

## 1. North Carolina Layer (Primary Coverage — 3 Verified Items)

### 1. NC DEQ Finds 'Significant Deficiencies' in Chemours' Assessment and Corrective Action Plan Under the 2019 Consent Order

- **Published / Action Date:** `2026-11-09`
- **Verified Source (`NC-01` — NC Department of Environmental Quality (NC DEQ)):** [https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10) · [Secondary Source](https://tossonanalytics.com/regulatory-watch)
- **NC Counties / Watersheds:** Lower Cape Fear region — Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, Harnett, Hoke, New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, Pender
- **Docket / Rule:** 2019 Consent Order — On/Offsite Assessment & Corrective Action Plan
- **Compounds Tracked:** All PFAS associated with Fayetteville Works (incl. HFPO-DA/GenX & Attachment C fluoroethers)

In comments sent November 9, 2026, NC DEQ detailed the problems in Chemours' On and Offsite Assessment and Corrective Action Plan and laid out what compliance with the 2019 Consent Order and North Carolina law requires: a full assessment of contaminated soil and groundwater throughout the Lower Cape Fear region, a revised Corrective Action Plan that restores contaminated groundwater, expansion of the plan to all PFAS associated with Fayetteville Works rather than a limited subset, and an evaluation of how the facility's remaining emissions affect groundwater. Reporting by Inside Climate News (November 11, 2026) adds that DEQ's comments run 36 pages, find that Chemours could not justify how it assessed the public-health and environmental risks of PFAS exposure, and cite missing data, substandard sampling methods, flawed analyses, and outdated toxicological data for GenX; DEQ had rejected an earlier draft Corrective Action Plan in 2020 as inadequate. A Chemours spokesperson responded that the company is reviewing the comments in detail and will respond through the established process.

> **Why It Matters:** This is the regulator's own deficiency finding, not an advocacy allegation: nearly a decade after GenX became public, neither Chemours nor DEQ knows the full extent of Lower Cape Fear contamination — and the sampling boundaries, filtration eligibility, and municipal planning that thousands of households depend on all rest on the assessment DEQ just found deficient.

### 2. DEQ's Air Quality Division Separately Flags Chemours' Emissions Data, Lab Analysis and Transparency — With Immediately Required Actions

- **Published / Action Date:** `2026-11-09`
- **Verified Source (`NC-01` — NC Department of Environmental Quality (NC DEQ)):** [https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10) · [Secondary Source](https://tossonanalytics.com/regulatory-watch)
- **NC Counties / Watersheds:** Fayetteville Works airshed — Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, Harnett, Hoke, New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, Pender
- **Docket / Rule:** NC DEQ Division of Air Quality — Fayetteville Works air emissions
- **Compounds Tracked:** Air-emitted PFAS (completeness of the facility's compound inventory in dispute)

In a separate action the same day (November 9, 2026), DEQ's Division of Air Quality sent Chemours correspondence expressing concerns about data collection, laboratory analysis, and transparency regarding air emissions from Fayetteville Works, including a list of immediately required actions. As reported by Inside Climate News (November 11, 2026), Division Director Michael Abraczinskas wrote that Chemours has also failed to identify all of the PFAS compounds it is emitting into the air from the plant — emissions the division links to continuing groundwater contamination. A plan addressing the state's air concerns is reported due by February 2027.

> **Why It Matters:** Air deposition is the pathway that spread GenX from one plant across a region's wells. If the facility's emissions inventory is incomplete, the groundwater assessment built on it cannot be complete either — the two November 9 letters are one story, not two.

### 3. The Consent Order at the Tap: ~27,500 Wells Sampled, ~10,500 Families Offered Filtration, a Barrier Wall Over a Mile Long

- **Published / Action Date:** `2026-11-09`
- **Verified Source (`NC-02` — NC DEQ — GenX Investigation & Consent Order Portal):** [https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/key-issues/genx-investigation/genx-information-residents](https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/key-issues/genx-investigation/genx-information-residents) · [Secondary Source](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NCDEQ/bulletins/4323a10)
- **NC Counties / Watersheds:** Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, Harnett, Hoke, New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, Pender
- **Compounds Tracked:** HFPO-DA (GenX) & Attachment C Fluoroethers
- **Screening Benchmark:** 10 ppt individual / 70 ppt combined Consent Order threshold (screening)

DEQ's November 9 release also inventories what the Consent Order has delivered since 2019: Chemours has stopped discharging highly contaminated wastewater from Fayetteville Works; built a subsurface barrier wall more than a mile long and a treatment system to keep PFAS out of the Cape Fear River; installed systems to treat contaminated groundwater and stormwater; installed pollution controls on facility air emissions; sampled approximately 27,500 household wells across southeastern North Carolina; and offered clean drinking water via filtration systems to approximately 10,500 families whose wells are polluted, alongside agreements to connect additional homesites to municipal water systems. DEQ's point is the contrast: source control and household protection advanced, while the long-term assessment and cleanup plans stalled.

> **Why It Matters:** For well owners inside the footprint, these are the programs they actually touch — sampling eligibility and filtration offers continue to operate even as the long-term cleanup dispute escalates, and DEQ's figures are the current official count of both.


## 🚰 North Carolina Private Wells Corner: 27,500 Wells Sampled Under the Consent Order — and the Three Pathways for Everyone Outside It

- **Verified NC Well-User Population Basis:** 2.4 million (NC DHHS OEEB conservative baseline) to 3.3 million / 35% of state population (USGS 2010 baseline, Maupin et al. 2014 via MacDonald Gibson et al. 2017, PMC5744693); ~2.8 million via CWFNC 2005 public-supply subtraction estimate.

UCMR 5 did not sample a single private well, leaving North Carolina's unregulated domestic well population — conservatively estimated at 2.4 million residents by the NC Department of Health and Human Services (NC DHHS) and at approximately 3.3 million residents (35% of the state, the second-highest total in the nation after Pennsylvania) in USGS water-use data analyzed by MacDonald Gibson et al. (Environ. Health Perspect., 2017) — entirely outside federal tap-water surveillance. As Clean Water for North Carolina Senior Advisor Hope Taylor observed in a November 2026 exchange with Tosson Analytics, subtracting reported public-water-supply customers from total state population already yielded ~2.8 million residents in 2005 before two decades of rural well permitting. For North Carolina well owners seeking testing today, three verified pathways exist: (1) Free Consent-Order Sampling across 10 Counties: Under the Chemours Consent Order enforced by NC DEQ, Parsons Environment and Infrastructure — reachable at (910) 678-1100 or (910) 678-1101 — is the sole authorized sampler. DEQ reported on November 9, 2026 that approximately 27,500 household wells have been sampled under the Consent Order and approximately 10,500 families offered filtration to date, following NC DEQ's March 10, 2025 expansion (+150,000 residences across Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, and parts of Harnett and Hoke) and September 11, 2025 Lower Cape Fear expansion (+14,000 residences across New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, and Pender); wells screening at ≥10 ppt GenX or any listed Attachment C PFAS (or ≥70 ppt combined) qualify for replacement water, RO/GAC filtration, or municipal connection at Chemours' expense; (2) Statewide Free RO or Up to $3,500 Reimbursement: Where no responsible party is identified and private-well results exceed a state or federal standard, NC DEQ's PFAS Alternate Water Program (backed by the Bernard Allen Memorial Emergency Drinking Water Fund, funded with an additional $10.2M in the enacted July 2026 state budget) provides a free under-sink RO system or up to $3,500 in first-come, first-served reimbursement for qualifying treatment installed after November 2019; and (3) Certified Private Lab Panels: Outside free programs, county health departments test bacteria and nitrates ($0–$60) but do not run PFAS; homeowners must order an accredited EPA Method 533 (25 compounds) or Method 537.1 (18 compounds) panel directly from a certified lab (starting around $380 at NC private labs and $300–$600 nationally) and screen their lab report against EPA's MCL benchmarks (4.0 ppt PFOA/PFOS, 10.0 ppt GenX/PFNA/PFHxS).

### 1. TSCA Section 8(a)(7) PFAS Reporting Window Opens January 31, 2027 for 2011–2022 Manufacture and Imports

- **Published / Action Date:** `2026-04-13`
- **Verified Source (`US-01` — Office of the Federal Register (NARA / GPO)):** [https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/13/2026-07062/modification-to-the-start-of-the-submission-period-for-perfluoroalkyl-and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/13/2026-07062/modification-to-the-start-of-the-submission-period-for-perfluoroalkyl-and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances) · [Secondary Source](https://www.epa.gov/pfas/previous-actions-address-pfas)
- **Docket / Rule:** EPA-HQ-OPPT-2020-0549 · Document 2026-07062 (91 FR 18786)
- **Compounds Tracked:** All PFAS (2011–2022 lookback)
- **Screening Benchmark:** One-time TSCA §8(a)(7) reporting obligation

Under EPA's April 13, 2026 final rule (Document 2026-07062, 91 FR 18786), the modified submission window for TSCA Section 8(a)(7) opens January 31, 2027. Manufacturers and importers — including importers of finished articles — must report PFAS manufactured or imported during the 2011–2022 lookback period, covering fluoropolymers and product categories that earlier reporting regimes never captured. The one-time reporting obligation is the federal government's most comprehensive PFAS commerce inventory to date.

> **Why It Matters:** North Carolina manufacturers, importers, and downstream users are inside this lookback. The submissions will shape future federal and state PFAS priorities — and companies that cannot reconstruct a decade of supply-chain records by January face a compliance problem, not a paperwork exercise.

## 🔬 Research Radar: New Peer-Reviewed Literature

### Paper 1: Serum per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) concentrations and anti-spike SARS-CoV-2 IgG levels following COVID-19 vaccination: A cross-sectional study in three communities with elevated PFAS exposure

- **Authors:** Sarah Rhea, David Collier, Michael Cuffney, C. Suzanne Lea, Nadine Kotlarz, and Jane A. Hoppin
- **Journal & Year:** *International Journal of Hygiene and Environmental Health*, 2026 (Vol. 273, 114755; epub Feb 4, 2026)
- **Identifiers:** DOI `10.1016/j.ijheh.2026.114755` · PMID `41643486` · Source ID `US-07`
- **Full-URL Links:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41643486/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41643486/) · [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheh.2026.114755](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheh.2026.114755)

NC State and East Carolina University researchers in the GenX Exposure Study measured serum PFAS and anti-spike SARS-CoV-2 IgG in 330 vaccinated adults from three Cape Fear River Basin communities with elevated PFAS exposure (data collected June–November 2021). For the five PFAS detected in >85% of samples, estimates of the PFAS–antibody relationship were modest and inconsistent in direction across communities; the only statistically significant estimate was a small positive association for PFHpS in the Lower Cape Fear River region. The authors found no consistent evidence linking higher serum PFAS to a lower COVID-19 vaccine antibody response.

> **Scope & Claim Boundary (§1.3):** Cross-sectional design using 2021 samples and a single antibody measurement per participant; a null/inconsistent association in this cohort does not settle vaccine-response questions for other populations, vaccines, or exposure windows. The authors call for longitudinal studies with serial antibody measurements.

### Paper 2: Adapting to PFAS contamination of private drinking water wells near a PFAS production facility in the US Atlantic Coastal Plain of North Carolina

- **Authors:** Tiffany J. VanDerwerker, Detlef R. U. Knappe, and David P. Genereux
- **Journal & Year:** *Water Environment Research*, 2024 (Vol. 96, Issue 8, e11091)
- **Identifiers:** DOI `10.1002/wer.11091` · PMID `39072849` · Source ID `US-07`
- **Full-URL Links:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39072849/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39072849/) · [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/wer.11091](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/wer.11091)

NC State researchers used water-quality data from more than 100 wells near the Chemours Fayetteville Works plant — where PFAS are present in over 7,000 drinking-water wells — to test whether deeper aquifers can serve as alternate supplies. GenX concentrations decreased with depth through the four-aquifer sequence (surficial, Black Creek, Upper Cape Fear, bedrock), tracing roughly 40 years of atmospheric emissions downward. Detailed sampling of nine deep wells (143 parameters) found only eight exceedances of drinking-water standards and nine health-advisory exceedances (all sodium), and the authors conclude the bedrock aquifer may be a viable alternative for some residents.

> **Scope & Claim Boundary (§1.3):** Site-specific hydrogeology: the depth pattern reflects this aquifer sequence and emission history, and the authors flag arsenic treatment needs at some deep wells and long-term bedrock sustainability as open questions. Not a blanket recommendation to drill deeper — NC's regulatory response at the time mentioned deeper wells only for nonresidential users.
