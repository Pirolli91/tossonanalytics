## ⚡ The Top Line: EPA's Completed UCMR 5 Dataset Locks In North Carolina's Statewide Baseline: 47 Counties and 120 Public Water Systems Screen Above Federal PFAS MCL Benchmarks

- **Defining Number:** **120 NC public water systems (across 47 counties, led by Nash County at 122.5× the 4.0 ppt PFOA/PFOS benchmark)**
- **Primary Sources (`NC-17`, `US-03`):** [https://tossonanalytics.com/dashboard](https://tossonanalytics.com/dashboard) · [https://www.epa.gov/dwucmr/data-summary-fifth-unregulated-contaminant-monitoring-rule](https://www.epa.gov/dwucmr/data-summary-fifth-unregulated-contaminant-monitoring-rule)

EPA's final Fifth Unregulated Contaminant Monitoring Rule (UCMR 5) release — rebuilt into the Tosson NC PFAS Dashboard on November 6, 2026 — completes the 2023–2026 national occurrence survey across 68,633 North Carolina analytical results from 70 counties. In North Carolina, 177 public water supply (PWS) sites recorded quantifiable PFAS detections and 120 sites across 47 counties recorded at least one maximum concentration above EPA's 2024 Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) benchmarks. Because UCMR 5 is an occurrence and screening survey rather than a running-annual-average compliance determination under the Safe Drinking Water Act, these 120 sites represent an engineering and capital-planning queue ahead of the April 26, 2027 initial monitoring deadline under 15A NCAC 18C .1540.

## 1. North Carolina Layer (Primary Coverage — 5 Verified Items)

### 1. NC DEQ Expands Chemours Private-Well Testing to ~164,000 Additional Residences Across 10 Counties in 2025–2026 Orders

- **Published / Action Date:** `2025-09-11`
- **Verified Source (`NC-01` — NC Department of Environmental Quality (NC DEQ)):** [https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2025/09/11/deq-requires-chemours-expand-well-testing-lower-cape-fear](https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2025/09/11/deq-requires-chemours-expand-well-testing-lower-cape-fear) · [Secondary Source](https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2025/03/10/nc-deq-directs-chemours-significantly-expand-well-testing)
- **NC Counties / Watersheds:** Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, Harnett, Hoke, New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, Pender
- **Compounds Tracked:** HFPO-DA (GenX) & Attachment C Fluoroethers
- **Screening Benchmark:** 10 ppt individual / 70 ppt combined Consent Order threshold (screening)

Across two consecutive enforcement directives under the 2019 Chemours Consent Order, the North Carolina Department of Environmental Quality (NC DEQ) expanded residential private-well sampling eligibility by approximately 150,000 residences up to 30 miles from Fayetteville Works on March 10, 2025 (covering Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, and newly added portions of Harnett and Hoke counties) and by approximately 14,000 additional residences in the Lower Cape Fear on September 11, 2025 (covering New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, and Pender counties). Authorised sampling is conducted at zero cost to homeowners by Parsons Environment and Infrastructure at (910) 678-1100 / (910) 678-1101. Any private well screening at or above 10 ppt for HFPO-DA (GenX) or any listed Attachment C PFAS — or 70 ppt combined — triggers Chemours-funded municipal water connection, whole-house granular activated carbon (GAC), or under-sink reverse osmosis (RO) filtration plus maintenance.

> **Why It Matters:** More than 164,000 North Carolina households across 10 counties gained eligibility for free certified PFAS well screening and polluter-paid filtration, closing a major rural exposure gap without drawing down state taxpayer funds.

### 2. NC DEQ PFAS Alternate Water Program Offers Statewide Free Under-Sink RO or Up to $3,500 Reimbursement via Bernard Allen Fund

- **Published / Action Date:** `2026-07-20`
- **Verified Source (`NC-03` — NC DEQ Division of Waste Management — PFAS Treatment System Assistance Program):** [https://www.deq.nc.gov/pfas-treatment-system-assistance-program](https://www.deq.nc.gov/pfas-treatment-system-assistance-program) · [Secondary Source](https://www.deq.nc.gov/pfas-alt-water-program-application-72026/download?attachment)
- **NC Counties / Watersheds:** Statewide (All 100 NC Counties, non-responsible-party wells)
- **Compounds Tracked:** PFOA, PFOS, PFNA, PFHxS, HFPO-DA (GenX), PFBS (Hazard Index)
- **Screening Benchmark:** 4.0 ppt PFOA/PFOS · 10.0 ppt PFNA/PFHxS/GenX · HI 1.0 (screening)

Administered by NC DEQ's Division of Waste Management through the Bernard Allen Memorial Emergency Drinking Water Fund — which received an additional $10.2 million allocation in the enacted July 7, 2026 North Carolina state budget — the PFAS Treatment System Assistance Program provides statewide relief for private well owners and small community wells (serving ≤10 residences) where no responsible party is identified. Homeowners whose well water screens above federal drinking-water standards (4.0 ppt for PFOA or PFOS; 10.0 ppt for PFNA, PFHxS, or HFPO-DA/GenX; or a 1.0 Hazard Index mixture with PFBS) qualify for a free state-installed under-sink RO system (including one-time maintenance) or up to $3,500 in first-come, first-served reimbursement for a qualifying point-of-use RO, whole-house point-of-entry system, or municipal connection installed after November 2019.

> **Why It Matters:** For the 47 North Carolina counties where UCMR 5 public-system screening hits signal broader aquifer vulnerability outside the 10-county Chemours footprint, this little-known program is the primary state financial backstop for private well treatment.

### 3. NC EMC Advances Groundwater PFAS Rulemaking (15A NCAC 02L .0202) and Evaluates Record on NPDES Discharge Monitoring Rules (15A NCAC 02B .0512 / 02H .0923)

- **Published / Action Date:** `2026-09-25`
- **Verified Source (`NC-04` — NC DEQ & Environmental Management Commission (EMC)):** [https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2026/03/10/updated-public-hearings-scheduled-proposed-pfas-monitoring-minimization-rules](https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2026/03/10/updated-public-hearings-scheduled-proposed-pfas-monitoring-minimization-rules) · [Secondary Source](https://www.deq.nc.gov/accessdeq/rules-regulations/deq-proposed-rules)
- **NC Counties / Watersheds:** Statewide (NPDES Dischargers, POTWs & Groundwater Aquifers)
- **Docket / Rule:** 15A NCAC 02B .0512 / 02H .0923 & 15A NCAC 02L .0202
- **Compounds Tracked:** PFOA, PFOS, HFPO-DA (GenX) + 1,4-Dioxane companion rules

North Carolina's regulatory posture remains split between source monitoring and ambient water standards following the June 15, 2026 close of public comments on the Environmental Management Commission's (EMC) proposed surface-water monitoring and minimization rules (15A NCAC 02B .0512 and 15A NCAC 02H .0923). Those proposed rules would require industrial NPDES direct dischargers, POTWs with pretreatment programs, and significant industrial users to sample for PFOA, PFOS, and GenX within 60 days using EPA Method 1633A, monitor quarterly for one year, and submit minimization plans, though the Southern Environmental Law Center (SELC) has criticized the package for omitting enforceable numeric effluent limits. Concurrently, in September 2026, NC DEQ informed its Science Advisory Board that permanent groundwater standards for PFOA, PFOS, and GenX under 15A NCAC 02L .0202 continue through rulemaking while surface-water numeric standards are reworked.

> **Why It Matters:** With EPA having withdrawn its 2022 federal NPDES PFAS permitting guidance memorandum in August 2026, North Carolina's pending EMC rules represent the state's sole regulatory mechanism to force upstream industrial disclosure before utilities face the 2029/2031 drinking-water compliance window.

## 🚰 North Carolina Private Wells Corner: The 2.4 Million to 3.3 Million North Carolinians Outside UCMR 5: Methodology, Free Consent-Order Sampling & the $3,500 Bernard Allen Fund Route

- **Verified NC Well-User Population Basis:** 2.4 million (NC DHHS OEEB conservative baseline) to 3.3 million / 35% of state population (USGS 2010 baseline, Maupin et al. 2014 via MacDonald Gibson et al. 2017, PMC5744693); ~2.8 million via CWFNC 2005 public-supply subtraction estimate.

UCMR 5 did not sample a single private well, leaving North Carolina's unregulated domestic well population — conservatively estimated at 2.4 million residents by the NC Department of Health and Human Services (NC DHHS) and at approximately 3.3 million residents (35% of the state, the second-highest total in the nation after Pennsylvania) in USGS water-use data analyzed by MacDonald Gibson et al. (Environ. Health Perspect., 2017) — entirely outside federal tap-water surveillance. As Clean Water for North Carolina Senior Advisor Hope Taylor observed in a November 2026 exchange with Tosson Analytics, subtracting reported public-water-supply customers from total state population already yielded ~2.8 million residents in 2005 before two decades of rural well permitting. For North Carolina well owners seeking testing today, three verified pathways exist: (1) Free Consent-Order Sampling across 10 Counties: Under the Chemours Consent Order enforced by NC DEQ, Parsons Environment and Infrastructure — reachable at (910) 678-1100 or (910) 678-1101 — is the sole authorized sampler, following NC DEQ's March 10, 2025 expansion (+150,000 residences across Cumberland, Bladen, Robeson, Sampson, and parts of Harnett and Hoke) and September 11, 2025 Lower Cape Fear expansion (+14,000 residences across New Hanover, Brunswick, Columbus, and Pender); wells screening at ≥10 ppt GenX or any listed Attachment C PFAS (or ≥70 ppt combined) qualify for replacement water, RO/GAC filtration, or municipal connection at Chemours' expense; (2) Statewide Free RO or Up to $3,500 Reimbursement: Where no responsible party is identified and private-well results exceed a state or federal standard, NC DEQ's PFAS Alternate Water Program (backed by the Bernard Allen Memorial Emergency Drinking Water Fund, funded with an additional $10.2M in the enacted July 2026 state budget) provides a free under-sink RO system or up to $3,500 in first-come, first-served reimbursement for qualifying treatment installed after November 2019; and (3) Certified Private Lab Panels: Outside free programs, county health departments test bacteria and nitrates ($0–$60) but do not run PFAS; homeowners must order an accredited EPA Method 533 (25 compounds) or Method 537.1 (18 compounds) panel directly from a certified lab (starting around $380 at NC private labs and $300–$600 nationally) and screen their lab report against EPA's MCL benchmarks (4.0 ppt PFOA/PFOS, 10.0 ppt GenX/PFNA/PFHxS).

## 📊 Tosson Data Corner: Why 66% Co-Occurrence in UCMR 5 Changes Treatment Selection for North Carolina's 120 Above-Benchmark Systems

- **Tracker Snapshot:** `68,633 NC UCMR 5 results · 177 PWS sites with detections · 120 sites (47 counties) above MCL benchmarks · 66% national mixture co-occurrence`
- **Verified Sources (`NC-17`, `US-03`):** [https://tossonanalytics.com/dashboard](https://tossonanalytics.com/dashboard) · [https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-08/ucmr5-data-summary_0.pdf](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-08/ucmr5-data-summary_0.pdf)

In Tosson Environmental Analytics' final November 6, 2026 UCMR 5 build, 177 North Carolina public water systems across 70 counties recorded quantifiable PFAS detections, and 120 systems across 47 counties had at least one single-sample result above EPA's 2024 MCL benchmarks (4.0 ppt for PFOA and PFOS; 10.0 ppt for PFHxS, PFNA, and HFPO-DA/GenX), with Nash County recording the highest single-sample screening ratio at 122.5× the benchmark. Crucially, UCMR 5 measures occurrence against screening benchmarks — it does not establish SDWA MCL compliance or a regulatory violation, which is calculated from running annual averages at entry points to the distribution system. When paired with EPA's July 2026 national UCMR 5 summary (EPA 815-S-26-007) showing that 66% of detection locations nationwide (4,286 of 6,499 locations) contain mixtures of two or more PFAS, the engineering takeaway for North Carolina utilities is clear: single-compound breakthrough modeling is insufficient where short-chain fluoroethers (GenX, PMPA, PEPA) or PFHxS co-occur with PFOA and PFOS, making site-specific Rapid Small-Scale Column Tests (RSSCTs) essential before locking in granular activated carbon (GAC) or anion-exchange resin vessels.

- **Verified Source (`GL-01` — European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) & OECD PFAS Portal):** [https://www.echa.europa.eu/-/echa-supports-pfas-restriction-with-targeted-derogations](https://www.echa.europa.eu/-/echa-supports-pfas-restriction-with-targeted-derogations) · [Secondary Source](https://echa.europa.eu/hot-topics/perfluoroalkyl-chemicals-pfas)
- **Jurisdiction:** European Union (ECHA REACH) & OECD
- **Docket / Rule:** ECHA/NR/26/17 (RAC Final Opinion / SEAC Final Expected Late 2026)
- **Compounds Tracked:** Universal PFAS Class (>10,000 substances), PFHxA & Fluoropolymers

The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) announced on March 26, 2026 (ECHA/NR/26/17) that its Committee for Risk Assessment (RAC) adopted its final scientific opinion on March 2, 2026 and its Committee for Socio-Economic Analysis (SEAC) agreed on its draft opinion on March 10, 2026 evaluating the five-nation universal REACH restriction proposal covering >10,000 PFAS. Following a 60-day consultation on SEAC's draft opinion that closed May 25, 2026, SEAC is scheduled to adopt its final opinion by year-end 2026 ahead of European Commission rulemaking. In the interim, the EU's targeted REACH restrictions on undecafluorohexanoic acid (PFHxA) in textiles and food packaging took effect in April 2026 and the EU ban on PFAS in portable fire extinguishers and firefighting foams entered into force in October 2026, complemented by the OECD's November 2025 146-page synthesis report on the global fluoropolymer lifecycle.

> **Why It Matters:** Multinational textile, electronics, and fluoropolymer supply chains operating in North Carolina must comply with EU PFHxA and firefighting-foam phase-outs now in effect, accelerating upstream substitution well ahead of US federal TSCA rules.

### Paper 1: Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Ether Acid (PFEA) Concentrations in Indoor Dust are Higher in Homes Closer to a Fluorochemical Manufacturing Facility

- **Authors:** Susie Proctor, Jane A. Hoppin, Sharon Zhang, Heather M. Stapleton, Detlef R. U. Knappe, and Nadine Kotlarz
- **Journal & Year:** *Environmental Science & Technology*, 2025 (Vol. 59, Issue 14, pp. 7200–7210)
- **Identifiers:** DOI `10.1021/acs.est.4c07043` · PMID `40162913` · Source ID `US-07`
- **Full-URL Links:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40162913/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40162913/) · [https://pubs.acs.org/esthag/article/59/14/7200/3662959/Per-and-Polyfluoroalkyl-Ether-Acid-PFEA](https://pubs.acs.org/esthag/article/59/14/7200/3662959/Per-and-Polyfluoroalkyl-Ether-Acid-PFEA)

Researchers from NC State University and Duke University quantified 48 PFAS in settled indoor house dust collected from 65 homes of GenX Exposure Study participants living near the Chemours Fayetteville Works facility in North Carolina. Eight PFAS — including facility-associated fluoroethers PFO2HxA, PMPA, and PEPA alongside 6:2 diPAP, 6:2/8:2 diPAP, PFPrA, PFDS, and N-EtFOSAA — were detected in more than 90% of home dust samples (with PFOA detected in 74% at a median of 4.2 ng/g and linear PFOS in >80% at a median of 3.2 ng/g), and concentrations of six facility-linked PFEAs (PEPA, PMPA, PFMOAA, PFO2HxA, HFPO-DA/GenX, and Nafion byproduct 2) increased significantly with residential proximity to the plant.

> **Scope & Claim Boundary (§1.3):** Demonstrates an indoor residential dust accumulation pathway associated with proximity to Fayetteville Works air/environmental emissions; does not quantify the relative contribution of dust ingestion versus drinking-water intake to participant blood serum concentrations.

### Paper 2: Forecasting and Hindcasting PFAS Concentrations in Groundwater Discharging to Streams near a PFAS Production Facility

- **Authors:** Craig R. Jensen, David P. Genereux, D. Kip Solomon, Detlef R. U. Knappe, and Troy E. Gilmore
- **Journal & Year:** *Environmental Science & Technology*, 2024 (Vol. 58, Issue 40, pp. 17926–17936)
- **Identifiers:** DOI `10.1021/acs.est.4c06697` · PMID `39319827` · Source ID `US-07`
- **Full-URL Links:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39319827/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39319827/) · [https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.4c06697](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.4c06697)

Using sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) and tritium/helium-3 (3H/3He) groundwater age-dating beneath tributary streams near the Chemours Fayetteville Works plant in the Cape Fear River Basin, NC State and partner hydrogeologists detected PFAS in sub-streambed groundwater recharged up to 43 years earlier (dating back to 1978), with PMPA (mean 498 ng/L) and HFPO-DA/GenX (mean 229 ng/L) dominating the analytical profile. Lumped-parameter convolution modeling indicates that legacy groundwater recharge from atmospheric deposition will continue discharging PFAS into Cape Fear tributary streams at concentrations exceeding current drinking-water benchmarks through at least 2050 to 2060.

> **Scope & Claim Boundary (§1.3):** Applies specifically to surficial aquifer baseflow discharging to streams in the Fayetteville Works atmospheric deposition footprint under modeled recharge assumptions; does not evaluate engineered pump-and-treat or barrier-wall capture efficiency on the plant boundary itself.

### Paper 3: Predictions of groundwater PFAS occurrence at drinking water supply depths in the United States

- **Authors:** Andrea K. Tokranov, Katherine M. Ransom, Laura M. Bexfield, Bruce D. Lindsey, Elise Watson, Danielle I. Dupuy, Paul E. Stackelberg, Miranda S. Fram, Stefan A. Voss, James A. Kingsbury, Bryant C. Jurgens, Kelly L. Smalling, and Paul M. Bradley
- **Journal & Year:** *Science*, 2024 (Vol. 386, Issue 6723, pp. 748–755)
- **Identifiers:** DOI `10.1126/science.ado6638` · PMID `39446898` · Source ID `US-05`
- **Full-URL Links:** [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39446898/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39446898/) · [https://www.usgs.gov/publications/predictions-groundwater-pfas-occurrence-drinking-water-supply-depths-united-states](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/predictions-groundwater-pfas-occurrence-drinking-water-supply-depths-united-states)

US Geological Survey (USGS) scientists trained an extreme gradient-boosting machine-learning model on 1,238 untreated groundwater samples collected between 2019 and 2022 across 24 PFAS (where 37% of samples contained at least one detectable PFAS) to predict occurrence at domestic and public drinking-water supply depths nationwide. The model estimates that 71 million to 95 million people in the conterminous United States (~25% of the population) potentially rely on untreated groundwater with detectable PFAS prior to any water treatment, with urban land use, well depth, and subsurface recharge rate serving as the strongest predictors.

> **Scope & Claim Boundary (§1.3):** Models probability of any PFAS detection (at method reporting limits) in untreated source groundwater at supply depths; does not measure treated tap water or predict exceedance of specific 4.0 ppt or 10.0 ppt EPA MCLs at individual domestic wells.
